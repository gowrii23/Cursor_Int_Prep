Write-ahead logging, drawn out

Four diagrams in the order things happen: a commit, a crash and recovery, SQLite's WAL mode, and the same idea replicated across machines.

The one rule everything rests on: a changed data page may reach disk only after the log records describing that change are on disk. In LSN terms: pageLSN ≤ flushedLSN before the page is written.

Say it in an interview

  1. Commit latency is the cost of one sequential fsync. Group commit spreads that cost across many transactions.
  2. Data pages are flushed lazily (no-force), and dirty pages may be evicted before commit (steal). That is why recovery needs both redo and undo.
  3. Checkpoints limit how long recovery takes and how much log you keep.
  4. Things to watch in production: WAL disk usage, checkpoint lag, replica or reader lag that blocks log truncation, and fsync latency (p99).
